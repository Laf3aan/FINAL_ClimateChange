What is Climate Change?

Climate change is a long-term shift in Earth's average temperatures and weather conditions.

This is a major global issue—here is why it matters.

Why is Climate Change Bad?

As noted by NASA Science, “The average surface temperature has risen about 2 degrees Fahrenheit since the late 19th century.”

Human Health and Lives ❤️

  • 13 Million Deaths: Environmental factors already take around 13 million lives globally each year.
  • 250,000 Extra Deaths: The WHO estimates climate change will cause 250,000 additional deaths per year between 2030 and 2050 from heat stress and disease.
  • $2–4 Billion Costs: Direct health damage costs will reach up to $4 billion annually by 2030.
  • 3.6 Billion Vulnerable: Nearly half of the global population lives in areas highly vulnerable to climate impacts.

Temperature and Weather ⛈️

  • 1.4°C Hotter: Global temperatures are ~1.4°C (2.5°F) above pre-industrial levels, driving intense heatwaves.
  • Stronger Storms: Warmer oceans feed heavier rainfall and more destructive hurricanes.

Water and Sea Levels 🌊

  • 2 Billion Affected: Over two billion people currently lack safe drinking water due to shifting climate patterns.
  • 0.5% Usable Water: Only 0.5% of Earth's water is usable freshwater, and climate shifts endanger this supply.
  • 8 Inches Higher: Global sea levels have risen by ~8 inches over the last century, threatening coastal cities.
  • 30% Carbon Absorption: Oceans absorb 30% of human CO2 emissions, increasing acidity and destroying reefs.

What Causes Climate Change?

Main Human Causes ⚒️

  1. Burning Fossil Fuels: Coal, oil, and gas release massive amounts of carbon dioxide.
  2. Deforestation: Cutting down forests stops trees from absorbing CO2 and releases stored carbon.
  3. Agriculture & Livestock: Livestock releases methane, while synthetic fertilizers emit nitrous oxide.
  4. Industrial Manufacturing: Producing steel, cement, and plastics releases heavy industrial gases.
  5. Waste & Landfills: Decomposing trash in landfills releases potent methane gas.

Natural Factors 🌳

  1. Volcanic Eruptions: Volcanoes release CO2, though significantly less than human activity.
  2. Solar & Orbital Changes: Natural shifts in Earth's orbit influence long-term climate cycles.

How Can We Fix It?

Actionable steps toward a sustainable future:

Key Solutions 💡

  • Transition to Renewable Energy: Shift global power grids to solar, wind, and hydroelectric sources.
  • Protect & Restore Forests: Reforestation efforts naturally extract carbon dioxide from the atmosphere.
  • Adopt Sustainable Transport: Upgrade to electric vehicles and expand public transportation networks.

What Organization helps with the Climate Change?

There are actually a few organizations that helps with the climate change. One of them is UNEP.

What is UNEP?

  • United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP) is an international organization created by the United Nation which its core mission is to find solutions to the triple planetary crisis. As the leading global authority on the environment, the institution helps its 193 Member States to foster climate stability, live in harmony with nature and forge a pollution-free future, supporting the achievement of all 17 SDGs.

What is SDG?

  • The Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), also known as the Global Goals, are a collection of 17 interlinked global objectives to serve as a shared blueprint for peace and prosperity for people and the planet. Adopted by all United Nations Member States in 2015 as part of the 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development, these goals set a 15-year deadline to achieve a sustainable future by the year 2030.
  • SDG 13 talks about the problem of Climate Change.

How can UNEP achieve their goal of SDG 13?

The UN Environment Programme achieves its goal of SDG 13 (Climate Action) by driving decarbonization, promoting adaptation, and providing scientific assessments.

Mitigation and Decarbonization

  • Phasing out fossil fuels: UNEP works on "ending leaded fuel" and "phasing out coal" while pushing toward net-zero emissions.
  • Tracking emissions: The agency helps build transparent tracking tools, such as creating a "global public database of methane emissions".

Adaptation and Resilience

  • Ecosystem-based approaches: UNEP promotes nature-based solutions to help vulnerable communities adapt to climate hazards and protect natural ecosystems.
  • Strengthening capacity: It assists countries in integrating climate risk management and resilience into national policies and local planning.

Science-Policy Interface

  • Providing data: UNEP publishes flagship studies like the Emissions Gap Report and Adaptation Gap Report to track global progress.
  • Informing decisions: By analyzing environmental threats and climate finance shortfalls, UNEP guides global leaders in meeting the Paris Agreement temperature targets.
